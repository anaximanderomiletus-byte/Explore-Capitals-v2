Geography is one of those subjects where people are confidently wrong all the time. The mistakes are so widespread that they have become conventional wisdom, repeated in conversations, news broadcasts, and even textbooks. Correcting them is not about being pedantic. It is about building an accurate mental model of the world.

Here are the most common geography mistakes and the real facts behind them.

## "Holland" Is Not the Netherlands

This might be the single most common geographic misnomer in the English-speaking world. Holland is not a country. It is a region within the Netherlands, consisting of two provinces: North Holland and South Holland. These provinces contain Amsterdam, Rotterdam, and The Hague, which is why foreigners associate "Holland" with the entire country. But the Netherlands has 12 provinces, and calling the whole country Holland is like calling the United States "California."

The Dutch government officially asked people to stop using "Holland" in 2020 as part of a rebranding campaign. The correct name is the Netherlands, or in Dutch, Nederland.

## Africa Is a Continent, Not a Country

This one should not need saying, but it does. Africa is a continent of 54 countries with a combined population of over 1.4 billion people. It spans from the Mediterranean coast of Morocco to the southern tip of South Africa, covering deserts, rainforests, savannas, mountains, and coastlines.

Treating Africa as a monolith erases the enormous diversity within it. Nigeria and Botswana have almost nothing in common economically. Morocco and Madagascar share a continent but are separated by 7,000 kilometers and radically different cultures, languages, and climates. The phrase "in Africa" is almost always too vague to be meaningful, much like saying "in Asia" to describe something happening in both Japan and Saudi Arabia.

## Central America Is Not a Continent

There are seven continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia (or Oceania), Europe, North America, and South America. Central America is not one of them. It is a region within North America, connecting Mexico to South America.

The seven countries of Central America, which are Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, and Panama, are geographically and politically part of North America. The confusion arises because "North America" is often informally used to mean only the United States, Canada, and Mexico. But the continent extends all the way through Central America to the Colombian border.

Similarly, the Caribbean islands are part of North America, not a separate continent.

## Europe and Asia Are One Landmass

Europe and Asia sit on the same continuous landmass, often called Eurasia. The division between them is cultural and historical, not geographic. There is no ocean separating Europe from Asia. The conventional boundary runs along the Ural Mountains, the Caspian Sea, the Caucasus Mountains, and the Black Sea, but these are conventions, not natural continental boundaries.

This creates some interesting edge cases. Turkey straddles both continents, with its largest city, Istanbul, literally divided by the Bosphorus strait. Russia is by far the largest country in both Europe and Asia, with about 77 percent of its territory in Asia but about 77 percent of its population in Europe. Georgia, Armenia, and Azerbaijan sit in the Caucasus region and are claimed by both continents depending on the source.

## Greenland Is Not as Big as It Looks

On a standard Mercator map, Greenland appears to be roughly the same size as Africa. In reality, Africa is about 14 times larger. Greenland's area is approximately 2.17 million square kilometers. Africa's is about 30.37 million square kilometers.

The distortion comes from the Mercator projection, which stretches landmasses near the poles to preserve navigational angles. This makes high-latitude regions like Greenland, Canada, Russia, and Antarctica appear far larger than they are relative to equatorial regions. It is the single most misleading feature of the most common world map.

To see accurate relative sizes, look at a Peters projection or use an interactive tool like thetruesize.com, which lets you drag countries to different latitudes to see how their apparent size changes.

## The Sahara Is Not the Biggest Desert

When people hear "desert," they picture sand dunes and extreme heat. But a desert is technically defined as a region that receives very little precipitation, typically less than 250 millimeters per year. By this definition, the largest desert on Earth is Antarctica, which receives so little snowfall that it qualifies as a polar desert. At 14 million square kilometers, it is larger than the Sahara's 9.2 million.

The Arctic is the second largest desert. The Sahara is third. Deserts are defined by dryness, not heat.

## Istanbul Is Not the Capital of Turkey

Istanbul is the largest city in Turkey and one of the most famous cities in the world. It was the capital of the Byzantine Empire and the Ottoman Empire. But it has not been the capital of the Republic of Turkey since 1923, when Mustafa Kemal Ataturk moved the capital to Ankara.

Ataturk chose Ankara deliberately. It is centrally located in Anatolia, far from the borders and the legacy of the Ottoman imperial capital. The move symbolized a break from the past and a new national identity. Over a century later, people still guess Istanbul.

## There Are More Than Four Oceans

Many people were taught that there are four oceans: Atlantic, Pacific, Indian, and Arctic. But in 2000, the International Hydrographic Organization recognized the Southern Ocean, the body of water surrounding Antarctica, as the fifth ocean. National Geographic officially recognized it in 2021.

The Southern Ocean is defined by the Antarctic Circumpolar Current, the strongest ocean current on Earth, which flows continuously around the continent and creates a distinct ecological and climatic zone. It is the fourth largest ocean by area, larger than the Arctic.

## Russia Spans 11 Time Zones

Most people know Russia is the largest country in the world. Fewer realize just how far it stretches. Russia spans 11 time zones, from Kaliningrad on the Baltic Sea (UTC+2) to Kamchatka in the Pacific (UTC+12). When it is noon in Moscow, it is 9 PM in Vladivostok.

The country covers more than 17 million square kilometers, which is larger than the surface area of Pluto. It borders 14 countries and two oceans. Its east-west span is so vast that it is closer to Alaska than Alaska is to the nearest US state, Hawaii.
