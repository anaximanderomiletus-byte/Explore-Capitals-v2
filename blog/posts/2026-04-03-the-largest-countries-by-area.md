Massive Russia covers a mind-bending 17.1 million square kilometers, stretching completely across eleven distinct time zones from Kaliningrad on the freezing Baltic Sea all the way to Vladivostok on the roaring Pacific. It is easily nearly twice the size of the second-largest country, Canada, and almost an unbelievable 50 times the size of Germany! On any map, Russia completely dominates the northern hemisphere. But most of that massive space is freezing Siberian taiga, deep permafrost, and empty tundra where absolutely almost no one actually lives.

The totally fascinating relationship between a country's massive total area and its actual usable land, its real population, and its economic power is one of the most surprising, instructive disconnects in all of geography.

## The Top Five

The five absolute largest countries by total area are massive Russia (17.1 million square kilometers), vast Canada (10.0 million), the sprawling United States (9.8 million), massive China (9.6 million), and huge Brazil (8.5 million). Together, these five giants account for roughly an incredible 40 percent of the world's total land area.

But their populations tell a completely different, totally wild story. China holds a massive 1.4 billion people. The United States holds 340 million. Brazil holds 216 million. Russia holds just 144 million. Canada holds a tiny 40 million. Canada, officially the second-largest country on Earth, actually has fewer people living in it than the single US state of California! Its vast, freezing northern territories are largely just completely uninhabited tundra and thick boreal forest.

## Russia: The Empty Giant

Russia's population is heavily, tightly concentrated in a relatively narrow band of territory sitting safely west of the Ural Mountains, heavily centered on the massive cities of Moscow and Saint Petersburg. East of the Urals lies massive Siberia, which easily accounts for roughly 77 percent of Russia's total land area but holds only about a tiny 27 percent of its total population.

The incredible emptiness of Siberia is completely a product of its brutal climate. Freezing winter temperatures in central Siberia regularly drop well below a deadly minus 40 degrees! Permafrost—ground that stubbornly remains totally frozen year-round—covers roughly a massive 65 percent of all Russian territory. Building basic infrastructure directly on permafrost is incredibly expensive and totally unreliable: paved roads wildly buckle, deep foundations dangerously shift, and massive oil pipelines require insanely thick, special insulation. The legendary Trans-Siberian Railway, finally completed in 1916, remains one of the absolute most ambitious engineering projects ever undertaken just to connect this vast, empty space.

## Canada: North of the Treeline

Canada's entire population tightly hugs the southern border directly with the United States. Roughly an incredible 66 percent of all Canadians live within just 100 kilometers of the US border! The freezing northern territories—Yukon, Northwest Territories, and Nunavut—together cover more than a massive 3.9 million square kilometers but are home to incredibly fewer than 120,000 total people.

Massive Nunavut alone is easily larger than all of Mexico but has a tiny population of roughly just 40,000, mostly tough Inuit communities. The freezing territory's average temperature is minus 10 degrees Celsius, and much of it sits completely above the harsh Arctic treeline, where absolutely no trees can even grow. Supplying these totally remote communities relies entirely on dangerous seasonal sea lifts and incredibly expensive air freight.

## Algeria: Africa's Largest

Algeria officially became Africa's absolute largest country when South Sudan finally separated from Sudan in 2011. Its massive 2.4 million square kilometers make it the tenth largest country in the entire world. But roughly an incredible 80 percent of Algeria is completely swallowed by the scorching Saharan desert, and the vast majority of its population lives tightly packed in the narrow, beautiful coastal strip right along the Mediterranean Sea.

The scorching Saharan interior is home to scattered oasis communities, massive gas extraction facilities, and some of the absolute most spectacular, towering desert landscapes on Earth, including the ancient Tassili n'Ajjer rock art sites and the massive Grand Erg Oriental sand sea. But it is fundamentally totally empty land, far too hot and completely dry for large-scale human settlement.

## Australia: The Outback

Australia covers a massive 7.7 million square kilometers, making it the sixth largest country and the absolute only one that is actually also an entire continent. Its vast, dusty interior, colloquially known as the rugged Outback, is extremely arid or semi-arid land that stubbornly supports massive cattle stations, huge mining operations, and ancient Indigenous communities, but very few actual people.

Roughly an incredible 86 percent of all Australians live in dense urban areas, and the five absolute largest cities (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, and Adelaide) are all coastal. The tight concentration is extreme: significantly more people live tightly packed in the greater Sydney metropolitan area than in the entire massive state of Western Australia, which covers a staggering one-third of the entire continent!

## Small Countries, Big Impact

At the totally other extreme, some of the absolute most globally influential countries are geographically incredibly tiny. The United Kingdom covers just 243,000 square kilometers, roughly exactly the size of the US state of Michigan. Japan covers just 378,000 square kilometers but boasts the world's third-largest massive economy. South Korea, sitting at just 100,000 square kilometers, is technically smaller than freezing Iceland but has an incredible 50 times its population and one of the absolute most advanced, powerful economies on Earth.

Singapore, sitting at just a tiny 733 square kilometers, is easily one of the wealthiest and absolute most globally connected nations on the planet. Its entire total land area would easily fit directly inside the boundaries of most standard national parks! Massive size, clearly, is totally not destiny.

## Key Takeaways
- **Canada is mostly empty:** Canada is the second-largest country on Earth, but it has fewer people than the state of California because most of its land is freezing tundra.
- **Russia's frozen half:** Siberia makes up 77 percent of Russia's massive land area, but only 27 percent of its population actually lives there because of the deadly cold.
- **Algeria's desert takeover:** Algeria is the largest country in Africa, but 80 percent of it is completely covered by the scorching, uninhabitable Sahara Desert.
- **Australia hugs the coast:** Almost 90 percent of Australians live strictly on the coast because the massive interior Outback is too dry and hot for large cities.
- **Size isn't everything:** Tiny countries like Japan, South Korea, and Singapore have massive, powerful global economies despite having very little actual land.
