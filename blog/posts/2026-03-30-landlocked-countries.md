Open a world map and trace the coastlines. Every continent is ringed by nations with ports, beaches, and direct access to the ocean. But look inland and you will find 44 countries that are entirely surrounded by other countries, with absolutely no coastline. 

These landlocked nations face a unique set of geographic challenges. Being landlocked affects everything from trade and military strategy to tourism and national identity. Here is why it matters.

## The Trade Problem

Over 80 percent of global trade moves by sea. Massive container ships carry everything from electronics to grain across oceans for a fraction of the cost of air or land transport. Countries with ports can load and unload goods directly. Landlocked countries cannot.

Every single import and export from a landlocked country must cross an international border by land before reaching a port. This adds cost, time, and political risk. If a neighboring country closes its border, raises transit fees, or simply has bad roads, the landlocked nation's entire economy can suffer. 

## Africa's Landlocked Belt

Africa has more landlocked countries than any other continent: 16 in total. They stretch across the continent in a broad belt from Mali in the west to Ethiopia in the east, and down through Zambia in the south.

Many of these countries are among the world's poorest, and their lack of a coastline is a big reason why. Studies show that landlocked countries trade about 60 percent less than comparable coastal nations. 

Ethiopia is the most populated landlocked country in the world, with over 120 million people. It actually lost its coastline when Eritrea gained independence in 1993. Since then, Ethiopia has relied almost entirely on Djibouti, a small coastal neighbor, to ship its goods.

## Europe's Wealthy Exceptions

Not all landlocked countries are poor! In Europe, several landlocked nations are among the wealthiest on the planet. Switzerland, Luxembourg, Liechtenstein, and Austria all lack coastlines but have thriving economies.

The secret is infrastructure and good neighbors. European landlocked countries are surrounded by stable, wealthy nations with excellent railroads and highways. Plus, they use major rivers like the Rhine and the Danube to ship heavy cargo directly to the sea. 

Central Asian landlocked countries, like Uzbekistan and Tajikistan, do not have this luxury. They are separated from the nearest ocean by thousands of miles of rough terrain. Uzbekistan and Liechtenstein even share a rare geographic title: they are "doubly landlocked," meaning they are completely surrounded by other landlocked countries!

## Military and Strategic Challenges

Historically, navies required coastlines. Landlocked countries could not build powerful fleets, which limited their military strength. 

More importantly, landlocked countries are strategically vulnerable. They must depend on their neighbors to connect with the outside world. For example, Bolivia lost its coastal territory to Chile during a war in the 1880s. To this day, Bolivia still maintains a navy that trains on a lake, and the country continues to fight for its right to access the Pacific Ocean!

## The Psychology of Being Landlocked

There is also a cultural side to being landlocked. Countries with coastlines develop maritime traditions: sailing, ocean fishing, and beach tourism. The sea becomes a part of their national identity.

Landlocked nations develop different relationships with their landscapes. Nepal's identity is tied to the towering Himalayas. Switzerland is defined by the Alps. Mongolia's vast, grassy plains shape its culture. Tourism in these countries focuses on mountains, lakes, and wildlife rather than beach resorts.

## Key Takeaways
- **No ocean access means higher costs:** Landlocked countries must rely on their neighbors' ports, which makes importing and exporting goods much more expensive.
- **Africa has the most:** There are 16 landlocked countries in Africa, more than any other continent.
- **Rivers can replace coastlines:** Wealthy landlocked nations like Switzerland use massive river networks to ship goods to the sea.
- **Double landlocked:** Only two countries in the world (Uzbekistan and Liechtenstein) are entirely surrounded by other landlocked nations.
- **A different kind of culture:** Without oceans, landlocked nations build their cultural identities around mountains, plains, and lakes instead of maritime traditions.
